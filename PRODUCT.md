# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Mesh Connect is for college students who need the right people to make a project, prepare for a hackathon, or get help with a practical problem. They normally arrive with a limited amount of time, a half-formed idea, and an uneven set of skills. The main job is to make the next useful collaboration obvious: see who fills a skill gap, express interest safely, and start a focused conversation.

Administrators are a secondary audience. They need a small, dependable view of reported content and the user base; the student workflow remains the center of the product.

## Product Purpose

Mesh Connect is a campus collaboration platform that connects students through complementary skills rather than popularity or friend circles. Success means a student can make a credible profile, understand why a suggested person is relevant, form a mutual match, and begin a conversation without needing to leave the application.

## Positioning

Find the person who makes your project possible, not just another person who does the same thing.

## Brand Personality

Clear, capable, and quietly optimistic. The product should feel like a well-organized campus studio: practical enough for serious project work, warm enough to make reaching out feel low-risk, and never like a dating app or a gamified social feed.

## Anti-references

Do not make it look like a dating product, a generic AI dashboard, a crypto/finance product, or a dark neon developer tool. Avoid visual noise, fake metrics, excessive cards, gradient text, glassmorphism, and social-media engagement bait.

## Design Principles

1. **Explain the recommendation.** A score without a reason is not useful. Every suggestion should make the complementary skill visible.
2. **Keep the next action obvious.** Interest, message, reply, and profile-edit actions must be clear without an onboarding manual.
3. **Make collaboration feel purposeful.** Use project and skill context before decorative social cues.
4. **Earn visual emphasis.** Most of the interface is calm and neutral; color is reserved for meaningful status, primary action, and feedback.
5. **Protect student boundaries.** Show clear privacy, report, block, and authorization states. Never imply that a match allows unrestricted access.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The website targets WCAG 2.1 AA contrast and keyboard usability. It respects reduced-motion preferences, does not communicate state through color alone, uses semantic labels and visible focus states, and works at 320px mobile width through wide desktop layouts. Content and sample data should be inclusive of technical, creative, management, and non-technical student skills.
